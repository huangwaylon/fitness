# ききみみ クイズ: English listening game (as built, 2026-11-06)

The user asked for a game for several players. It's started from the HUD menu (originally せってい), syncs so everyone starts and plays together, and is for Japanese speakers learning English. It trains **listening** with spoken English sentences, using the Animal Crossing characters and world.

Draft 1 was reviewed by an English teacher (Japanese learners), a game/UX designer and an engineer, and this is the result, kept up to date as the feature's detailed reference: docs/design.md `### ききみみ クイズ` has the summary, docs/backend.md the wire format and docs/decisions.md the choices.

## The game

- **Host and speakers.** しずえ (Isabelle) hosts. Each question is spoken by a villager. Their icon bobs with an NH speech bubble that stays empty (「♪…」) and a thin progress bar the clip's length. The text is never shown before the reveal.
- **Length.** A game is **8 questions** at level 1, 2, 3 or 4 ("Level 4" below), 6 やま or 7 ほし ("Levels 6 and 7" below), or level 5, the アナウンス pack ("The アナウンス pack" below), or two スピード はしご ladders of 4 rungs ("スピード はしご" below).
- **Countdown.** 「よーい」 then 3-2-1 runs once, at the start (4 s: よーい for the first second). Each question then has a ~1.5 s lead-in: the villager pops in with a chime.
- **Answering.**
  - The choices show dimmed while the clip plays. They unlock when its first play ends.
  - One tap, final. Then the time limit runs from the end of the first play: the lobby's **「こたえる じかん」**, 20 / **30** (the default) / 60 びょう or なし (untimed: the question waits until everyone joined and here has answered; solo, until you answer). The host sets it and the others see it; each phone remembers its last as the next lobby's default (`DEVICE.kikimimiTimer`). It rides on the `open`'s seed (bits 2-3, "Sync"). Raised from 15 s at the user's request (2026-11-06: "increase timer for questions, also make it a setting you can set for a session").
  - 🔁 replay is allowed as often as you like. 🐢 slow is levels 1 and 2 only (slowed TTS undoes level 3's reductions).
- **Showing who answered.** Each player's avatar chip gets a ✓ when they answer, without showing what they chose (in the lobby a joined player's chip has no badge: PlayerChip `here`).
- **Reveal.** It starts once everyone joined has answered, or at the time limit. It is **untimed** (the user's, 2026-11-06: "dont set a timer when reviewing each round results, i want time to read"): it stays until the host taps つぎへ (solo: you do). Each other player taps **「よみおわった」** (📖 on their chip); the host's button reads 「つぎへ（よみおわった 1/2）」 but the host can always go on. With the host gone ("Sync"), every remaining player gets つぎへ instead. In it:
  - the right choice shows with ○, and a wrong pick with ×, so colour isn't the only signal;
  - the English sentence shows at ≥ 17 px with the key word (`focus`) highlighted, and the clip plays once automatically;
  - below it are the Japanese and the voice's accent (US; UK / AU only on a やま/ほし accent-exposure item);
  - the speaker reacts (✨ or 💦), and so does each player's chip.
- **Scoring.** A correct answer is **100**; wrong or timed out is 0. There's no speed bonus (it rewarded guessing) and, since 2026-11-06, no first-listen bonus either (the user's decision, curriculum §7.1): **「いっぱつ！」**, right with no replay or slow play, is a stamp on the reveal, on the review's rows and in the results' count (「いっぱつ 7もん」), never points. おなじへや never gives it (only the host's phone can replay). The wire's `s` bound stays 1040 so a phone from before still validates.
- **Results.**
  - しずえ reads them out like the morning broadcast: co-op (the default with several), the team's 「ふたりで n/8」; たいせん, a ranking with reactions ("Co-op and たいせん" below).
  - Then a **review list** of every sentence, the main learning moment. With any misses it **opens on them** (「まちがえた だけ」 on, the first missed row open; the user's, 2026-11-06: "for the ones i got wrong i want to review"). Each row folds: ○ / ×, the sentence, the いっぱつ stamp; tapped open, the English with its sound marks and the karaoke as ▶︎ plays it (a dialogue: its whole two-sided transcript), the marks' key, the Japanese, the answer, the trap's `note`, the skills it tripped, what I picked, ▶︎ and 🐢 (L1/L2).
  - **「まちがえた もんだいを もういちど」** (with any misses): a solo replay of just those items, a local deck in a lobby kept alone (nothing on the wire, as にがて れんしゅう), the reveal untimed, its answers recorded as practice (`p: 1`), so they feed the SRS like any (srs.ts: a miss is `again`, box 0, due at once; checked 2026-11-06).
- **Solo** works the same way, with one player.
- **Who can play.** A, B and the guest (G) can all host and join. There's no ベル or economy tie in v1.
- **「おなじへや」 (same room).** A lobby toggle, set by the host, makes only the host's phone speak. Without it, three phones a little out of sync sound like a chorus. Without it, suggest earphones.
- **No Animalese** before or over the English.

## Question types (v1)

- **`pic`**: tap the picture you heard, from 4 NH art choices. The words must be ones the players already know, or katakana traps (banana, museum, orange, tarantula, Halloween, vitamin, energy). The sentence carries the test, not an obscure noun. The distractors are heard in the sentence too, or sound close. Example: "I didn't catch a tuna, but I got a squid!" → squid.
- **`word`**: the sentence shows with a blank, and you pick the word you heard from **3 near-sound choices**. Every choice must fit the sentence, so meaning can't give it away.
  - L/R: light/right, fly/fry, rock/lock, glass/grass, collect/correct, play/pray.
  - Vowels: hat/hut/hot, cap/cup, bag/bug, fan/fun, full/fool, coat/caught (avoid in US voices).
  - Others: berry/very, sink/think, sea/she, food/hood, walk/work, can/can't (by vowel and stress, the US way), walk/walked, cat/cats, fifteen/fifty, thirteen/thirty.
  - Check each pair in American English (the voice's accent: US everywhere but the やま/ほし exposure items).
- **`num`**: prices in ベル, times and dates, as text choices with close distractors: 2:15 / 2:50, quarter past / to, 1,500 / 15,000 ベル, the 13th / 30th. These are built from **templates** (speaker × phrase × number), which also makes the bank big cheaply.
- **`reply`**: a villager asks something, and you pick the best reply from 3 written replies (like TOEIC Part 2). Example: "What're you up to?" → "Just fishing." This is level 2 and 3 only.
- **`mean`** (level 4): what the speaker really means, from 3 English paraphrases. The distractors are the literal reading, the opposite and a plausible wrong inference. Its `ask` is 「つまり、どういうこと？」; it renders like `reply`.
- **`gist`** and **`next`** (level 4, dialogues): over a 2-4-line dialogue between two villagers, what's going on or will happen (its own `ask`), and the best next line (「つぎの ひとことは？」). 3 English choices. "Dialogues" below.
- **Later (v2):** level 3 chunk tiles, a shared co-op best, たいせん scored against expectation (curriculum §11: needs per-player stages) (missed items come back through にがて れんしゅう: "Adaptivity" below).

**Mix per 8 questions.** L1: 3 pic, 3 word, 2 num. L2: 2 pic, 2 word, 2 num, 2 reply. L3: 1 pic, 2 word, 2 num, 3 reply. L4: 2 word, 2 num, 2 reply, 2 mean; once the dialogues are voiced, 2 word, 1 num, 1 reply, 2 mean, 1 gist, 1 next. L5 (アナウンス): 3 num, 2 gist, 2 mean, 1 reply. L6 (やま): 2 reply, 2 mean, 1 gist, 1 next, 1 word, 1 num. L7 (ほし): 2 mean, 2 gist, 1 next, 1 reply, 1 word, 1 num (what's meant and what's going on, 6 of 8; gist and next dialogues and ほし's single bulletins alike).

## Co-op and たいせん (docs/curriculum.md §14, increment 3; built 2026-11-06)

- **Co-op is the default** for a game of several (the user's choice). The results read **「ふたりで n/8」** (3 players: 「さんにんで」): a question counts for the team when **at least one player got it right**. Answers are hidden till the reveal, so nobody can lean on the other; the team covers each other's gaps, which suits two strong listeners with different weak spots, and a miss never costs the team anything the partner caught. Under it, **「そろって せいかい m もん」**: the questions every player in them got right (a player had a question when they answered it or were in the game as its clip began, their `join` heard before it: one who joined later doesn't hold back the ones before, one who let it time out does, on every phone alike; a soft, celebratory count). A player who leaves keeps their answers in both counts. Fair: the same on every phone (from the `join`s and `a`s heard; a lost `a` costs that question's credit on that phone only).
- **Never shame:** in co-op the others' chips show only ✓ (answered), never right or wrong, and no one's points; the reveal says how many got it (「2にん せいかい！」, none: 「むずかしかったね！」), never who. The results have no ranking; each phone shows its own tally (「じぶんは 8もん中 6もん」) and its own review list.
- **「たいせん」** is a lobby switch, set by the host (others see it): the points, the chips' ✓ / × at the reveal and the ranking as before. It rides on the `open`'s **seed's lowest bit** (1: たいせん; a new lobby's seed is even), so no new field and **no relay deploy** (the relay passes known fields only). Flipping it deals a new deck, which nobody has seen yet. A phone from before reads any seed as before (it ranks, as it always did). もういっかい keeps the mode. Expectation scoring (curriculum §11) waits for per-player stages.
- **Level names:** neutral places with ★ counts, never やさしい/むずかしい (curriculum §3): ★ はまべ, ★★ いりえ, ★★★ かわぐち, ★★★★ かわ・もり, ★★★★★ やま, ★★★★★★ ほし (a ★ a place on the ladder: bank.ts `levelRank`); the pack beside level 4, 📢 アナウンス (bank.ts `levelMark`). The UX build (docs/kikimimi-ux.md §4) renames them by ばしょ.
- Built: seed.ts `versus(seed)` (round/wire.ts reads it into the game's `f`), `open({vs})`, `configure({vs})`, the view's `vs`, `team {right, all, done}` (over the questions revealed) and `rightNow` (a reveal's count); Lobby's `data-kiki-vs`; Play's `data-kiki-team-now`; Results' `data-kiki-team`. Tests: session.test "co-op and たいせん", e2e kikimimi (co-op and たいせん, two players).

## Dialogues (docs/curriculum.md §14, increment 4; built 2026-11-06)

- **Content.** `dialogues.ts`: 24 dialogues (12 `gist`, 12 `next`), level 4, B1+ → B2, each from a scenario seed (§5.5): café orders going wrong (the wrong milk, a misheard name, a sold-out dish), a cancelled train, a GP receptionist's cancellation list, a friend cancelling, a coworker hinting for the meeting room, a landlord who won't commit, a neighbour's party, a gate change, a gym trainer's correction, a parcel left with the neighbour, store credit, a taxi mishearing Street for Road, "we should grab lunch", a video call breaking up, a friend on a diet saying no, a pharmacist and a long drive, an installer's "between eight and one", a service charge, "I'm easy", a hotel upsell, a bank scam call, a ferry called off. US, UK and AU pairs; reductions in `tts` (lemme, kinda, wanna, 'em), numbers written out.
- **Shape.** An item with `lines: {who, cast, en, ja, tts?}[]` (2-4, two villagers taking turns, each with their cast voice, bank4.ts CAST); `en` the lines joined (the `focus` is in one line), `speaker` / `cast` / `voice` the first line's, `ja` the right choice's Japanese, `note` the trap (≤ 40 字), `tags` as level 4's. The distractors: for `gist` a heard detail misplaced, the literal reading or the opposite; for `next` a repeated word or sound, the wrong role, an answer to what wasn't asked. Skills (tags.ts): `next` うけこたえ, `gist` だいじな ところ (both ほんね when the pragmatic layer is unsaid).
- **Clips: one per line**, `<item>-<n>` (n from 1; bank.ts `clipIds`). The audio script (v2) maps `clipItems(item)` over the bank: an item itself, or each line as an item of its own (its clip id, speaker, cast, `en`, `tts`, `ja`), so castOf, spokenText, the gate and the listen list need no dialogue case; `--list` shows the 84 line clips, and the next run voices them (then the dialogue gate opens by itself). In bank.ts, not clips.ts, so the script doesn't load clips.json. `voiced(item)` and `itemDur(item)`: the lines' lengths plus `LINE_GAP` (0.3 s) between them, which is the item's `dur` in the session's schedule (the host-clock design is unchanged: every phone computes the same summed length from clips.json).
- **Playing** (clipPlayer.svelte.ts `playSequence`): the lines' buffers decoded first, then each line through `playVoice` at its relay time (`sequenceStarts`: LINE_GAP after the one before; heard late, a line over is skipped and the one under way plays whole from now, never two at once). The reveal's replay, 🔁 and the review's ▶︎ play the whole sequence (no 🐢 at level 4). `talk` follows the turns from the schedule on every phone.
- **UI.** While it plays: both speakers' heads by the empty bubble, the one talking bobbing. At the reveal: the accent and the `note` in the bubble (level 4's other items now show their `note` too), the verdict and only the right choice and my pick (never under the sticky つぎへ), then the two-sided transcript (`Transcript.svelte`): the first speaker's lines left, the other's right (mint), each with its Japanese, the focus marked; a `next` item's answer closes it as a dashed 「つぎの ひとこと」 bubble from whoever's turn it is. The review list shows the transcript compact, a gist's answer under it.
- **The gate.** Dialogues have their own: deck.ts `mixes(clips)` gives level 4 its dialogue mix only once every line of every dialogue has a clip, so level 4's single items and the dialogues can be voiced in either order; until then neither the deck nor にがて れんしゅう (`inMix`) draws them. `playable` checks every clip of the items a level's mix draws.
- Tests: bank.test (24, 12 / 12, two villagers taking turns, cast and gender, Japanese, `tts` rules per line, the focus in one line, clip ids unique), deck.test (the dialogue mix, the gate both ways, clip ids, clipItems, itemDur, sequenceStarts), session.test (a sequence's summed length in the schedule on every phone); e2e kikimimi "dialogues" (a test hook, `__kiki.deck(ids)`, starts a solo game on them before their clips: both heads, the transcript and next line, the review's transcripts).
- As built, the session counts the deck again at `go` (a deck settled in the lobby: にがて れんしゅう's, which had kept the lobby's count and answers).

## Conditions (docs/curriculum.md §5.3, §9 and §14, increment 6; built 2026-11-06)

- **What.** A clip can carry a speech condition (`cond: {kind, level, bed?, under?}`, bank.ts `Cond`): a channel (`phone`, `signal`, `voicemail`, `pa`) or a noise bed (`cafe`, `street`, `station`, `party`), `light` or `heavy`, and a channel's bed behind it (a PA on a platform: `pa` + `station`; a friend calling from outside: `phone` + `street`). A dialogue's lines take the item's unless a line has its own. `conditions.ts condOf`: the clip's own, else a phone channel's light `phone` (a voicemail's `voicemail`; on a call tagged `signal`, light `signal` for each line that breaks off, `...`), a PA's light `pa`; video calls and face to face stay clean, and so does a listening file tagged `party` (a たちぎき: the game loops the party bed under the whole conversation, so its lines are mixed dry). Levels 1-3 never have one (their 🐢 is the clean take).
- **Wave 2 (W2-A, 2026-11-07).** `signal`, a line breaking up: the phone band and compressor, then dropouts (`conditions.ts dropGaps`: seeded by the clip, one every 1.4 s on average, 120-320 ms, none in the first 0.3 s or last 0.2 s; `dropOut`: 60 % silence, 40 % a packet-loss stutter, the 20 ms before repeated at 0.7× each time, 4 ms ramps), then G.726 at 24 kbps; heavy every 0.9 s, 150-450 ms, half stutters, line hiss and G.726 at 16 kbps. Its loose WER bound is 0.5 (heavy 0.6). Only the line that breaks off gets it, and the player asks again on the next line (conditions.test checks the files), so a dropout never falls on a memo answer. `party`: the fourth bed (16 reversed talkers, some close, in a living room, glasses, a 120 bpm groove from the next room; seed 2029, 121 KB). `under: true` puts a bed under the speech only (no lead-in or tail, a 40 ms fade), so a たちぎき line's clip keeps its length and the lines' `at` offsets hold. The bed then stops in the gaps between lines; W2-2 plays it continuously under the whole conversation instead (clipPlayer.svelte.ts, the bed shipped as static/audio/beds/party.<sha8>.m4a), so a party file's lines are mixed clean (condOf gives them none; tachi-pub-quiz's 8 re-mixed dry from the cached takes).
- **The settings** (`conditions.ts condPlan`, carried out by `scripts/kikimimi-audio.ts` with `scripts/tts/dsp.ts`):

  | condition | light | heavy |
  |---|---|---|
  | phone | band 300-3400 Hz (2× high/low-pass), compressor −22 dB 3:1, G.711 µ-law at 8 kHz | band 350-3200 Hz, −26 dB 4:1, line hiss at +32 dB, G.726 at 24 kbps |
  | voicemail | the caller's room first (RT60 0.35 s, wet 0.18), then the phone line | the same, then the heavy line |
  | pa | a two-tone chime (E6, C6; 1.5 s, 3 LU under the voice, 0.35 s gap), horn band 250-6000 Hz, −20 dB 3:1, hall RT60 1.0 s wet 0.28 | band 300-5000 Hz, −22 dB 4:1, hall RT60 1.6 s wet 0.4 |
  | bed (café, street, station) | **+15 dB** SNR | **+10 dB** |

  The SNR is integrated loudness (LUFS) of the speech over the bed's stretch; the bed starts 0.5 s before the speech and ends 0.35 s after (faded 0.25 s), from an offset picked by the clip's id (the beds loop). A call's bed sits at the source (before the phone line), a PA's at the listener (after the hall). Then the usual finish (−16 LUFS, limiter, AAC). These are curriculum §5.3's noise lever steps 1-2 (S1-S2, fair for levels 4-5); +5 dB waits for S3. The impulse responses are synthetic (seeded decaying noise, darker as it decays), convolved by ffmpeg `afir` as a plain convolution.
- **The beds** (`app/scripts/tts/beds/{cafe,street,station,party}.m4a`, committed, 24 s each, AAC 40 kbps, ~121 KB; `beds.json` says how each was made): `npm run audio:kikimimi-beds` (scripts/kikimimi-beds.ts) synthesises them, deterministic (seed 2026), so there's **no third-party audio and no licence to track**. The babble is our own TTS clips (levels 1-3's normal clips) reversed, started part-way in and layered 12-14 deep (4, far off, on the street), in a room (café, RT60 0.6 s) or a hall (station, RT60 2.0 s); with seeded shaped noise and simple synthesis: cup clinks, spoon taps and a steam wand (café), traffic rumble, cars passing and footsteps (street), walkers, a train arriving and air handling (station). A 1 s equal-power crossfade closes each loop. whisper hears no words in any of them.
- **Hashes and the cache.** A clean clip's source hash is unchanged (its master's). A conditioned one's is `conditions.ts condSource`: the master's hash with the condition's whole plan, its bed file's hash and a version, so a rebuilt bed or a changed setting re-mixes only the clips it touches, and re-mixing never re-speaks: the raw takes stay cached by the master's hash (`.cache/tts/raw/<master>-s<seed>.wav`). The two clips a person had re-rolled with `--seed 2000` keep that seed across runs (the script's `PINNED`; before, a default run would have re-spoken them).
- **QA.** The strict gate (whisper's content words, ECAPA similarity, re-rolls) runs on the clean take; the mixed clip only gets a loose check: whisper's WER over it ≤ 0.2 (light) or 0.3 (heavy), else it's flagged `cond-wer` for a person. ## スピード はしご (docs/curriculum.md §7 game 2, §14 increment 7; built 2026-11-06)

- **The mechanic.** One line of real daily life (B1+ → B2) heard four times, faster each time: **0.8×, 1.0×, 1.2×, 1.4×** of natural speech (ladder.ts `SPEEDS`; 1.0× = `NATURAL_RATE`, 4.3 syllables a second, curriculum §3's S1; 1.4× is 6.0, S4's fast casual). Each rung asks **something different**, so the climb is never the same question again: 1 the gist, 2 a detail, 3 a harder detail (a number, a condition, what to do), all three asked and answered **in Japanese** (nothing English to read before the next, faster listen); 4 **ことば** (dictation-lite): the line shown with its key chunk blanked and 3 near-sound forms of it that mean much the same ("The train's / was / got stuck", "I'd / I'll help her / I helped her", "you might get / might've got / you'd get"), so knowing the meaning by the top doesn't give it away: only the weak forms heard at 1.4× do. A game is 2 ladders (two speakers, two accents where the draw allows) = the quiz's 8 questions.
- **The climb and the score** (the learning-science review: a payout weighted to the top invites guessing). A rung is **climbed** when it's right **on the first listen** and every rung under it was climbed: the climb stops at the first miss, replay or time-out, and a later rung right after that earns nothing (ladder.ts `climb`). Points are a flat **100 a rung climbed** (`RUNG_POINTS`, never more for a higher rung; at most 800 a game, within the protocol's 1040), counted by the deck (session.ts `QuizDeck.score` → ladder.ts `ladderPoints`), so たいせん ranks by rungs climbed and every phone counts the same. 🔁 is allowed while answering but that rung can't be climbed (the rule is in the lobby). In おなじへや a replay counts against the host's own climb only (the others can't replay).
- **Reveals.** A rung below the top **moves on by itself 3 s after its reveal** (2026-11-06; the `ladder.rung` round's `timing.auto`, round/session.ts `WINDOWS.auto`: on every phone from the schedule, the host's beacon keeping the last word; the host's つぎへ may go sooner; no よみおわった; a timed-out rung 3 s after its limit; with the host gone too); the top rung's reveal stays untimed till つぎへ, as the quiz's. A rung's reveal says whether it climbed (「1.2× クリア！」 +100; 「もういちど きいたので のぼれず」; 「1.2× で ストップ」; 「のぼるのは つぎの はしごで」), and **below the top the line stays hidden and silent**: no English, no Japanese, no automatic replay, no 🔁 (game.svelte.ts `hidesLine`), so the next, faster rung is a listen of its own. The top rung's reveal is the quiz's: the line with its chunk marked, the sound marks and karaoke over the 1.4× clip, the Japanese, the accent, the note, and how high the ladder went (「1.0× まで のぼった！」). ## Content: `app/src/lib/kikimimi/`

- **`bank.ts`**: the item's shape and helpers; each item is `{id, lv, type, en, ja, speaker, voice, choices, answer, focus?, ask?, tts?}`. Authored in `bank1.ts` (levels 1-3), `bank4.ts`, `dialogues.ts`, `bank5.ts` and `content/`, all of them in `source.ts` `BANK` (the compiler's, the audio scripts' and the tests' input, never the app's: "Loading").
  - `speaker` is a villager id from roster.ts. Jock villagers say gym lines ("Ten more push-ups!"), since this is a fitness app.
  - Accents (2026-11-06, the user: American English first; docs/curriculum.md "American English first"): every level, unit and dialogue line is US, but for やま (6) and ほし (7), where at most 10% of a level may be UK or AU accent exposure, each such item tagged `accent-exposure` (bank.test "accent policy"). `voice` is the item's accent; the voice's gender is the speaker's (roster).
  - `ask` (pic, num) is a short Japanese question shown while the clip plays (「なにが つれた？」): a pic sentence names several of its pictures, so it says what to listen for.
  - `pic` choices are art keys (`critters/squid`, `finds/items/blue-umbrella`, `decor/kettlebell-0`…); `PIC_JA` gives each its Japanese label (VoiceOver, the review list).
  - `word` items' `focus` is the answer as it appears in `en` (the blank before the reveal). `reply` items' `ja` is the question → the right reply.
  - `tts` is what's spoken when it isn't `en` as written (level 4: reductions and numbers, docs/curriculum.md §9).
  - Aim for **≥ 40 items per level in v1**, templates included. Grow towards 80 or more. v1 has 46 / 49 / 49 (144 items); with level 4's 261, the pack's 30, やま's 200 and ほし's 115, the bank holds 750 (2026-11-06).
- **`deck.ts`**: `deck(seed, lv): Item[]` is deterministic, over the level's items as loaded (pool.ts `levelItems`). It follows the per-level mix, never puts the same type more than twice in a row, and shuffles the choices by the seed. Every phone builds the same deck from `{seed, lv}`, so no text goes on the wire. The returned items are copies: `choices` is the shuffled order and `answer` the right choice's index in it. Num items from the same template aren't drawn together while the pool allows.
- **`score.ts`**: pure scoring: `points(right)` (100 or 0) and `firstListen(right, replayed, same)` (the いっぱつ badge); the protocol's `QUIZ_MAX_S` stays 1040, an upper bound for older phones (deck.test checks it covers 8 × 100, and `QUIZ_QS` against the deck).
- **`clips.ts`**: `clipAt(id, slow?)`, where a loaded clip is in its pack (the pack's stem, offset and bytes: "Audio", Packs), and `clipDur(id)`, from clips.json as the loaded levels carry it.

## Sync: one new presence-only intent

`{kind:'quiz', g, op, …}` needs a `case 'quiz'` in `intentOf` (contract/live-protocol.ts) and `'quiz'` in `guestMaySend`. The relay's routing is unchanged: the intent is unstored and unacked, and goes to all others. That contract file is bundled into the Worker, so the relay deploys first (relay.yml on push to main). Client messages are ≤ 210 bytes, since the relay adds about 35 bytes and 256 is the cap.

| op | sender | fields | meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `open` | host | `g, seed, lv, same` | a lobby is open to the others (sent on みんなを さそう, never for a lobby kept alone); they see an invite banner (TTL: dropped if the host leaves or after 10 min). The seed's lowest bit is たいせん, its bit 1 スピード はしご, bits 2-3 こたえる じかん |
| `join` / `leave` | anyone | `g` | joining or leaving the lobby or game |
| `go` | host | `g, at` | the game starts at relay time `at` (now + 4 s) |
| `a` | player | `g, i, k, rp, s` | the answer to question i: choice k, `rp` whether it replayed, `s` the sender's running total |
| `skip` | host | `g, i, at` | the host's position: question i starts at `at` (i = 8: the results). Sent when it changes (つぎへ) and every 3 s while a game runs (the beacon) |
| `skip` | player | `g, i, at` | (2026-11-06) `i` the question the sender is in: its **よみおわった** (`at` that question's start); `i` the next one: its **つぎへ with the host gone** (`at` its now). A phone from before ignores a player's skip |
| `end` | host | `g` | the game is cancelled |

Field validation:
- `g`: `/^[a-z0-9]{4,8}$/`
- `lv`: 1-8 (4 もり, 5 アナウンス, 6 やま, 7 ほし, 8 room for the next level without a relay deploy; widened from 1-5 on 2026-11-06, a relay deploy; a phone offers only the levels it has every clip of)
- `i`: 0-7 (`a`), 0-8 (`skip`; 8 is the end, added 2026-11-05: the relay must be deployed for it)
- `k`: 0-3
- `s`: 0-1040 (8 × 130 from the first-listen bonus's days; now at most 800, the bound kept for older phones)
- `seed`: a 32-bit unsigned int. **Bit 0 たいせん, bit 1 スピード はしご, bits 2-3 こたえる じかん** (2026-11-06; seed.ts `TIMERS`, `timerOf`): 0 → 30 s (the default), 1 → 20 s, 2 → 60 s, 3 → none. No new field, so **no relay deploy** (the relay passes known fields only); every phone computes the same limit from the `open`, and changing it deals a new deck like たいせん. A phone from before reads any seed as a 15 s limit: it follows the host's beacons (a reveal may bounce back for a moment); update both phones

**The host is the clock** (2026-11-05, after a review reproduced drift with two sessions on one clock).
- Every phone has the deck and each clip's `dur`. Question 0 starts at `go.at`; within a question every phone computes the lead (1.5 s), the clip (`dur`) and the answer window (こたえる じかん, from the seed; none: Infinity). The reveal starts at the limit or once everyone joined and here has answered (each phone from the `a`s it heard; nothing sent), and it is **untimed** (2026-11-06): the next question's start is only ever known from a `skip`, so until one comes it's Infinity (session.ts `schedule`).
- The host's `skip` is its position: question i starts at `at`. The host sends it as it changes (its つぎへ; the end after the last question, i = 8) and every 3 s while a game runs. Every other phone **adopts** it, even for a question already under way there or one it got past by itself (it goes back). Starts never decrease: a start fixed cuts the questions before it short, and the phone shows the last question started, never an old one. A skip heard before this phone's own reveal ends the question there at once.
- A lost `skip` leaves that phone in its reveal for at most one beacon (3 s). A lost `a` is corrected by the next `s`; the reveal there waits for the time limit or the host's つぎへ.
- **よみおわった**: a player's skip to the question it's in (above). The host's view counts the others here who tapped it (`ready {n, of}`, each player's `ready`); a player's skip is never a position while the host is here.
- **The host gone** (its `leave`, or its presence gone for 15 s, `hostGrace`): nobody moves on by the clock. Every remaining phone shows つぎへ (`canNext`); a tap moves that phone on and sends its skip to the next question, which the others follow (rebased, only sooner), and a phone that sees the host gone a moment later follows it then (`advance`, `tick`). A host back after that adopts the players' position: a player hearing the host's beacon behind where they moved on answers with its skip, the host's phone adopts it and beacons it, and nobody goes back. A phone whose own socket is down ignores presence (who's here is unknown, nobody counts as gone).

**Late messages.** The host's times are kept as sent. A question whose clip began more than 5 s before its start was heard (a late `go`, a lock, a join mid-game) is sat out (`out`, not answerable); one heard sooner is played (its clip whole from then) on the host's answer window. A phone doesn't wait for itself on a question it sits out, and nobody waits for a player whose `join` came after its clip began. With こたえる じかん なし nothing is sat out: no hurry, the clip plays once from now as the phone comes in, and 🔁 as often as wanted.

**Re-sends.** When someone's presence comes back (and to everyone when this phone's own socket comes back) the host re-sends `open`, and once the game has started `go` plus its position; a player re-sends `join`. Everyone also re-sends its `a` for the question under way and, in a reveal, its よみおわった (and on hearing a `join`), so a phone back from a lock into an untimed reveal sees the others' answers and the host's count is right. So a phone back from a lock rejoins the host's clock at once. **A socket replaced unseen** (2026-11-07, `ws.hello` `rep`): a phone back within moments of a drop says hello before the room saw its old socket go, so the others hear no presence off and on; the host's re-sent `go` (in the lobby its unchanged `open`) is then the sign, and a player answers it with its `join`, game events, `a` and よみおわった (else an untimed round's host waited for ever).

## Progress & feedback (built 2026-11-05)

The user asked for "a mechanic for progress and improvement and learning tracking; recommendations and feedback on what to study and focus on". Both learners are B1+ (TOEIC 700+, listening already good), so the point is **which sounds and skills still trip you**, not a score. Decided defaults: each person's detailed results are theirs alone; only shared summaries reach the partner; co-op by default (no level next to the other's: docs/curriculum.md §11).

### What's measured (per person)

- **Every answer:** the item, right or not, the choice picked (the authored index, so distractors still diagnose: an L/R miss vs a vowel miss), first listen or not and the number of replays (🔁 and 🐢 alike), the game's level and whether it was にがて れんしゅう.
- **Sub-skills** (the bars). Each item has a primary skill (its type, or its first feature for `word`) and secondary ones (its other features, at half weight):

  | skill | label | from |
  |---|---|---|
  | `lr` | L と R | feature `l-r` |
  | `vowel` | にた ぼいん | `vowel` (hat/hut, ship/sheep) |
  | `cons` | にた しいん | `b-v`, `th`, `sh-s`, `f-h`, `ending` (cats/cat); a `word` item with no feature |
  | `reduce` | つながる おと | `contraction`, `weak-form`, `flap`, `yod`, `gonna` … `kinda`, `can-cant`, `t-glottal`, `ellipsis` |
  | `num` | すうじ | type `num`; `numbers`, `teen-ty`, `clock`, `self-repair` |
  | `detail` | だいじな ところ | type `pic` (the right word among others heard); `contrast`, `katakana` |
  | `reply` | うけこたえ | type `reply` with a literal or formulaic pragmatic layer; `idiom` |
  | `indirect` | ほんね | type `mean`; a `reply` whose pragmatic layer is indirect, a refusal, a hedge, understatement, sarcasm, humour, attitude or deception; `intonation` |

- **Tags.** Level 4 has `tags` already (bank4.ts). Levels 1-3 get a compact table keyed by item id (`kikimimi/tags.ts`: features plus, for replies, a pragmatic layer), with the same vocabulary plus S0's segmentals (curriculum §3): `l-r`, `vowel`, `b-v`, `th`, `sh-s`, `f-h`, `ending`, `katakana`, `contrast`, `clock`. bank.test checks every item has tags and every tag maps to a skill.
- **Mastery** of a skill (0-1): a recency-weighted (half-life 21 days), smoothed (prior 0.7 × 2 answers) mean of answer scores, **first listen right = 1, right after a replay = 0.6, wrong = 0** (curriculum §10: a first-play correct answer counts more). Shown from 3 answers (else 「まだ」).
- **Also per type, per accent (US / UK / AU; UK and AU only from the やま/ほし exposure items) and per level:** plain accuracy and first-listen accuracy, with counts.
- **The level estimate:** an Elo-style ability θ (curriculum §10: updates go to the learner, never the items). An item's difficulty is its level (L1 −1, L2 0, L3 1, L4 2) plus a type offset (pic −0.3, reply +0.2, gist and next +0.3, mean +0.4); after each answer θ += K × (score − expected), K 0.3 for the first 24 answers, then 0.15. The **comfortable level** is where θ predicts 80 % (θ − ln 4, on the levels' scale), capped at the hardest level played + 0.5, and shown as the curriculum's stage: below L2.5 S0 はまべ ★ (A2-B1), below L3.5 S1 かわ ★★ (B1+), below L4.5 S2 もり ★★★ (B2), else S3 やま ★★★★ (B2+). 「はかっている とちゅう」 until 16 answers.
- **Trend:** first-listen accuracy per game, the last 12 games (a sparkline), and the last 5 games' against the 5 before.
- **Consistency, never a streak:** the days played this Tokyo week (Monday first), 7 dots. A missed day costs nothing.

### Storage: one `listen` row per person per game

- **The row** (docs/backend.md "Events", `contract/listen.ts`): `listen {d, g, lv, n, ms, p?, r}` (plus `gm?`, `u?`, and a unit lesson's `l?` 1–5 and `tm?` 0–6, docs/curriculum-units.md §10: relay first, 2026-11-06). `d` the Tokyo day; `g` the game id (the session's, `[a-z0-9]{4,12}`); `lv` 1-8 (1-5 till 2026-11-06); `n` the players 1-3; `ms` the game's length (0-1 h); `p: 1` にがて れんしゅう; `r` 1-12 answers `[id, ok, pick, rp]` (`id` an item id, `ok` 0/1, `pick` the authored choice index or −1 timed out, `rp` replays 0-99). Only the questions I had (the session view's `had`: answered, or its clip began after I came in), so those before a mid-game join or slept through in a lock aren't in it, never counted as misses (progress.ts `listenAnswers`). ~200 bytes.
- **Why per game, not per day** (curriculum §12 proposed a day slot): a game is written once, at its results, with its own event id, so a retry is a duplicate and two phones of one person never overwrite each other's day; no slot and no merge on the record. A game left and joined again (the sheet closed and opened) writes a second row with the same `g`: the fold (progress.ts `gamesOf`) makes them one game, each answer once (the first kept), the minutes summed; the partner's summaries by `g` alike. ~3 games a day × 2 people is ~2,000 rows (~400 KB) a year. Never compacted (the record never compacts events).
- **Who sees it** (`contract/guest.ts rowsFor`, server-enforced, in every snapshot and delta): its owner the whole row; everyone else only the summary `{d, g, n, ms}` (that a game was played, when, how long, with how many: the together summary; never the level, the answers or the score), and `{u, stamped: 1}` for a unit's passed チェック (docs/curriculum-units.md §4); the guest and the members alike (2026-11-06: the guest gets the members' summaries, they get the guest's). The guest writes its own (`guestMayWrite`).
- **Relay change:** a new event type is refused by an older relay (`bad_request`), so the relay deploys before a phone sends one. An app from before it ignores the rows (`EVENT_TYPES`).
- **Offline:** the row goes through the write queue like every event (kept in localStorage until acked) and shows at once (the optimistic view); the snapshot cache keeps the acked ones. The phone also keeps its own copy of my last 300 games (`DEVICE.kikimimiGames`, `components/kikimimi/progress.svelte.ts`), so きろく has a game even when the record refused it (a relay from before the row: dropped without the save-failed toast, store/live.ts); the record's rows win by game id. Everything below is folded from the rows on the phone, never stored: the SRS state too (curriculum §12).

### Adaptivity: spaced repetition and にがて れんしゅう

- **The shared deck stays deterministic** (`deck(seed, lv)`): every multiplayer game and the normal solo game are as before. Personal adaptivity is solo only.
- **Cards** (curriculum §10: a chunk or a feature, never a clip): an **item card** for each item missed or answered only after a replay (in any game, multiplayer too: the per-player review set), and a **feature card** for each feature answered. Leitner boxes 0-6 with intervals 0 / 1 / 3 / 7 / 16 / 35 / 90 days: right on the first listen = good (up a box), right after a replay = hard (the box kept, at least 1), wrong = again (box 0, due at once). Folded from the answers in time order (`kikimimi/srs.ts`), deterministic, never stored.
- **「にがて れんしゅう」** (a solo mode, the lobby's): 8 questions from the levels this phone offers: due item cards first (most overdue, lowest box; at most 4), then items carrying the weakest due features (a different item from the one missed when there is one: another clip of the feature), then the weakest skills' items, never the same item twice. A recommendation's tap narrows it to one skill or one accent. Choices shuffled by the seed. Nothing on the wire (a lobby kept alone sends nothing); the session runs it as any solo game (`deckOf` returns the practice deck).

### Recommendations and feedback (`kikimimi/advice.ts`, pure)

- **「つぎは これを れんしゅう しよう」** (at most 3, each with a one-tap start):
  1. due cards ≥ 4: 「おさらい」 (にがて れんしゅう, every skill);
  2. the weakest skills with enough data (mastery < 0.75, or the lowest one below 0.85): 「{skill} を れんしゅう」 (にがて れんしゅう of that skill);
  3. a level nudge: the last 3 games at the current level ≥ 85 % right and a higher level offered: 「レベル N に ちょうせん」 (that level's game); ≤ 50 %: 「にがて れんしゅう」 before moving on;
  4. an accent ≥ 15 points below the others (≥ 6 answers each): 「{accent} の えいごに なれよう」 (にがて れんしゅう of that accent only);
  5. a skill the levels offered haven't measured (< 3 answers): 「{skill} を ためそう」 (last: at most 3 are shown, in this order).
- **しずえ's line after each game** (the results): the skill whose mastery rose most with this game (≥ +0.05): 「{skill}が のびてるよ！」, then the first recommendation: 「つぎは {skill}！」; all right: 「ぜんもん せいかい！ すごい！」; nothing yet: an encouragement.
- **Per-game feedback** (the results): 「つまずいた ところ」, chips of this game's missed features and skills with their counts (「L と R ×2」, 「-teen / -ty」), and each missed review row's feature label.

## マイク (docs/kikimimi-mic.md; the privacy rule, 2026-11-07)

- Off by default. Turned on per phone in ききみみ's せってい and off again there.
- Solo by default. The partner is involved only in ロールプレイ and あてっこ, and only when both players opted in: joining is consenting.
- **Recordings are never stored or synced.** A take lives in memory for its round and is dropped at the next round, on leaving, or when the app hides. It's never written to localStorage, IndexedDB, the Cache API, the record, R2 or the log, and never sent to the partner. The partner hears only your live voice in the room.
- **Speech to text runs on the phone first** (whisper in the page; the model is downloaded once after you agree, and can be deleted).
  - If Safari's recogniser is used, the audio may go to Apple.
  - If the relay is used (`POST /stt`, relay/src/sttRoute.ts), the audio goes to our relay and Cloudflare's Workers AI for the request only, and nothing is kept; the relay logs only sizes and times (`stt`), with a daily cap a person (300) and an off switch (`STT_OFF`).
  - The opt-in names the backend in use.
- **No transcript and no speaking score leaves the phone.** Mic games' rows carry minutes only. ロールプレイ shares only its ✓ per phrase, as the team's.
- **No ranking or leaderboard for speaking.** Mic modes are not in たいかい or きまぐれ.
- Heard text is never put into a log event or an `Error`. The probe's `mic.*` events are numbers and closed sets (contract/log.ts).
- The Permissions-Policy allows the microphone to our origin only.

**The probe (M0, built 2026-11-07):** せってい's last row 「マイク診断（テスト）」 opens `mic/MicProbe.svelte` (lazy) in the page's place: 環境 · マイクの許可 (and, on a later open, 開き直しの確認) · 再生と録音 per earphone route · Whisper (skipped until M0w) · Safari の音声認識 · サーバーで文字起こし, each speaking step on the 3 pinned sentences read aloud (lib/mic/probe/steps.ts `SENTENCES`), the WER computed on the phone (lib/mic/match.ts). 「結果を送信」 sends the `mic.*` events through the log; the heard text shows on screen only. The capture is lib/mic/probe/run.ts's (its own context, a ScriptProcessor: M1 brings the real capture). e2e: `e2e/kikimimi-mic-probe.spec.ts` in the `chromium-mic` project.

## Performance

- Nothing heavy loads before the sheet opens. The banner listener is a few hundred bytes.
- The art is the existing webp files, loaded lazily, plus level 4's painted scene backdrops (art/scene/, cached on first use). The island's render loop is idle under the sheet, as with other sheets.
- The deck's clips are fetched into the service worker's `kiki` audio cache (it outlives the sheet; nothing of the bytes is held in memory); in memory only an LRU of 4 decoded buffers (engine.ts `CLIP_CAP`), cleared when the game closes (`voiceSession(false)` → `clearVoice`). Measured 2026-11-06: the island's first load carries ~4 KB of ききみみ (the invite listener and host, the `listen` contract, the HUD pill, 4 strings, the cues; ~2 KB gzipped); the sheet's lazy chunk is 166 KB (50.2 KB gzipped, the index's 8.5 KB of it) and each level's content a chunk of its own ("Loading": 5-41 KB gzipped), きろく's 10 KB. During play nothing renders per frame but the karaoke (a frame loop only while a clip replays, re-rendering only when the word changes); the bars, the timer, the bob and the waves are CSS animations; the view refreshes at each phase's end (at least every 3 s).
