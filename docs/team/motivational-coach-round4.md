# Motivational Coach: Round 4 (open-ended saving, flat ¥50/fruit)

User input: no fixed destination, "save as much as possible", ¥520,000 used only as a scaling estimate, no sweet-drink habit, no Watch at night, super simple.
Verdict: **endorse the lead's flat-rate model**, with three small additions (a ladder that shows only the next rung, a Transfer Day ritual, and re-weighting the fruit without sleep).

## 1. Does an open-ended goal weaken motivation? Yes, unless the next step stays close.
- **Goal-gradient effect** (Hull 1932; Kivetz, Urminsky & Zheng 2006, coffee-card study): effort *speeds up as a goal gets close*. With no endpoint there is no gradient, so motivation flattens in the long middle (months 4–9).
- **Proximal sub-goals** beat distant ones (Bandura & Schunk 1981). Koo & Fishbach (2012, "small-area hypothesis"): early on people are motivated by *how much they've done*, and later by *how little is left*.
- **The ladder fixes it, if it's designed like this:**
  1. Show **only the next rung** on the Jar ("¥18,400 to an onsen weekend ♨️"), with a progress bar, not the whole ladder. The full list sits one tap deeper. Each rung is a fresh small goal, so the gradient restarts every few weeks.
  2. **Space the early rungs closer together** so the first one arrives in about 4–5 weeks: ¥30k → ¥50k → ¥100k → ¥150k → ¥300k → ¥500k → ¥600k+ ("dream rung").
  3. Passing a rung is a **mini celebration in the Sunday ceremony** ("You could take an onsen weekend now! 🎉 Keep climbing?"). Rungs are *previews, not spending prompts*. Spending happens only when both partners decide.
  4. **Optional soft pick** at the Season 2 opener: "Which rung sounds most exciting?" That rung gets a star, can be changed any time, and brings back a vivid goal without fixing a destination.
  5. Use Japan-specific rungs (onsen ryokan, Kyoto/Kanazawa, Okinawa, Taiwan/Korea, Hawaii) and let them edit the list.

## 2. Is ¥50/fruit + team ¥20 × lower score balanced?
- **Yes.** The team bonus is max ¥2,000 of ¥12,000 (≈ 17%), so 83% is personal and each person plainly owns "my part" (accountability). The team part is big enough to matter but too small for one partner to feel they're "costing us" (the guilt line is about 25%).
- Marginal values: while B is the lower partner, each of B's fruit is worth ¥70 (¥50 + ¥20) and each of A's is worth ¥50. That's fair, and helping the lower partner is literally worth money. Copy must still never say whose score set the team bonus.
- **Flat linear pay is better than the old 85-threshold + shortfall bank.** "1 fruit = ¥50" can be checked at a glance and done in your head. There's no cliff, so no what-the-hell effect, and every comeback week pays straight away, so no bank is needed. Strongly endorse.

## 3. Does losing pre-commitment matter? Somewhat. Replace it with a light ritual.
- Pre-funding gave loss-aversion framing (Patel 2016) and a visible stake. Now the stake is softer: unearned yen just stays in checking and gets spent on everyday life. For a cozy couple app that's acceptable, and the user explicitly wants open-ended saving.
- The real risk is **"earned but never moved."** If the transfer is skipped, the game is only points. Fixes:
  - **Transfer Day** on payday (25日) with one screen. Both partners tap "Moved it ✓" into a dedicated savings sub-account (a 目的別口座 or similar).
  - Optionally set a **standing auto-transfer of an estimate** (e.g. ¥35,000/month) plus a monthly top-up or refund. This is the closest thing to pre-commitment without locking money away.
  - An unmoved balance shows gently ("¥42,600 waiting to sail to savings ⛵"), never as debt.

## 4. Does linear pay up to 100 create an overtraining incentive? No.
- Every category is **capped at the person's own target**, plus the daily caps (90 active min, 1 strength day). 100 fruit means "hit every target", not "trained more". Extra volume pays ¥0.
- The new pressure is *perfectionism*: every missed fruit now visibly costs ¥50. Mitigations already in place: Cozy Days prorate targets, easy/recovery weeks still pay in full at their lower targets, and treats never cost fruit. Keep the copy pointing at "your usual week" (≈ 80) rather than 100.
- **Rest needs a home now that sleep is gone.** Keep the "≥ 1 rest day" rule as a condition inside the peach (≤ 6 active days count fully). It's invisible unless broken, and Juniper says "rest days grow strong trees too".

## 5. Recommended final numbers

**Treat Purse:** keep it **flat at ¥1,000 per person per week at ≥ 60** (paused counts). Don't make it a % of earnings. That would tie food treats to performance and blur the two pots. A flat "showed up" allowance stays guilt-free and is easy to see at a glance. Items ≥ ¥5,000 need both partners to say yes.
