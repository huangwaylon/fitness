# Nutritionist: personalized Season 1 plan

**User facts:** they cook at home on weekdays and sometimes eat out at weekends. Partner B works from home, sometimes skips lunch, and often snacks on potato chips. Partner A is in the office 3 days a week and eats lunch out on those days. **Neither drinks coffee or alcohol.**
**Unchanged:** 3 active habits per person, one evening check-in, the Pear at 15 of 21, answers private. Habits now differ per person.

## 1. Season 1 starter habits
| | Partner B (WFH) | Partner A (office 3 days) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **昼ごはんを食べる**: *Eat a real lunch* (staple + protein, by about 14:00) | **ランチは定食スタイル**: *Balanced lunch* (staple + main + vegetable side; eating out, conbini or home) |
| 2 | **おやつは果物・ナッツ・ヨーグルト**: *Fruit/nut/yogurt snack* (tick if today's snack included one; chips aren't banned) | **減塩デー**: *Salt-smart day* (left the broth, light on soy, read 食塩相当量) |
| 3 | **野菜を2食で**: *Veggies at 2 meals* (shared habit) | **野菜を2食で**: *Veggies at 2 meals* (shared habit) |

- **Why B's lunch habit comes first:** skipping lunch while training with a PT is the clearest under-fuelling risk in the whole plan. It also tends to drive the afternoon chip snacking.
  - The habit is about *eating*, not about what's eaten. Any real lunch counts.
  - B can set her own 12:30 lunch cue on the Watch. It's a user-scheduled reminder, not a nag.
- **The snack habit is positive, not a ban.** It ticks when a better option was *added*. Chips are still allowed. Small tips: buy 小袋 (snack-size bags) and eat from a bowl rather than the bag. No "no chips" rule, because a forbidden food tends to get more appealing.
- **A shared veggie habit** gives the couple one joint goal, and the weekday cooking makes it easy to hit together.

## 2. "No sweetened drinks" and 休肝日
**Not by default.** With no coffee, the main source is gone. Japanese bottled green tea, barley tea (麦茶) and oolong are unsweetened and fine.
**Ask the user:** does either of you drink juice, milk tea, Calpis, soda, sports drinks or energy drinks **3 or more times a week**? If yes, it replaces that person's weakest habit. If no, it's dropped, since there's no point rewarding something they already do. Sports drinks during long, sweaty sessions are always fine.
**休肝日 is dropped** for both, since neither drinks alcohol, and the app skips all alcohol copy for them.

## 3. Practical guides
**B: WFH lunches in 5 minutes or less** (cook extra at dinner = tomorrow's lunch):
- Leftovers from dinner (作り置き) + rice
- Natto rice + 減塩 instant miso + cherry tomatoes
- Frozen udon (3-minute microwave) + frozen vegetables + an egg; broth half-strength
- Tuna (水煮) or mackerel (サバ缶, bonus calcium) rice bowl + frozen broccoli
- Tofu + kimchi + rice
- Whole-grain toast + cheese + egg + fruit
- Unsweetened yogurt + banana + granola + nuts (for a light day)

**B: snack-swap list:** banana, mikan or apple; 素焼きナッツ (unsalted nuts, small handful); 小魚アーモンド (small fish and almonds); unsweetened yogurt with a drizzle of honey; cheese; frozen edamame; 炒り大豆 (roasted soybeans); 焼き芋 (roasted sweet potato). An onigiri if she's actually hungry: that's a meal signal, not a failure.

**A: choosing an office lunch:**
- **Teishoku:** grilled fish, ginger pork, chicken. Add a 小鉢 (small side dish); drink the miso soup or leave half.
- **Soba:** もり/ざる (cold, dipped) over かけ (in broth), dip lightly; add egg, wakame or tororo for protein.
- **Gyudon or curry chains:** add the salad + miso set (サラダセット).
- **Ramen days:** 野菜増し (extra vegetables), leave the broth (this also ticks 減塩).
- **Conbini combo:** onigiri + サラダチキン or a boiled egg + salad or 豚汁; for bento, pick one with a vegetable side and about 3 g salt or less on the label.

## 4. Weekday cooking and weekend eating out
- **No "balanced/home-cooked dinner" habit.** They already cook on weekdays, and rewarding an existing behavior wastes a slot. It can come back as a swap-in if the habit slips.
- **Weekends:** the Pear's 15-of-21 target leaves 6 ticks of slack, so weekend meals out never threaten it. The app says nothing about restaurant meals.
- **Treat Purse:** routine weekend meals out are just life, paid as usual, so a normal lunch out never turns into a "reward." The Purse is for *special* outings (yakiniku, a sushi counter) and non-food treats (onsen, karaoke, a cooking class), keeping at least half non-food.
