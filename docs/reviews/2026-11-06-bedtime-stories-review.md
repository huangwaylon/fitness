# Bedtime stories review (2026-11-06)

The panel: a native American editor, an EFL teacher and a native Japanese reviewer. We read both おやすみ ものがたり serials, `app/content/listen/story/st-johnny.yaml` and `st-cafe.yaml`, for a TOEIC 700+ listener and a B1+ partner.

## Verdict

**Both serials are ready to voice.** They're warm, gently funny and plainly American. The level suits B1+: the few B2 words (bait shop, hush-hush, outlive, handy) come with enough context. Segments read well aloud, the `tts` reductions are natural, the predictions match the next chapter and the phrase glosses are sound. We made small fixes only and kept every id.

## What we fixed

- **Japanese names:** Mabel is きぬよ in the roster, but the cafe summaries (ch3, ch4) said メイベル.
- **Japanese that didn't match the English:** in cafe ch3, it's Mabel who goes pale and Pekoe laughs (the summary had Pekoe going pale). In cafe ch1, Brewster gives three pieces of advice, not just 「小さく始めなさい」, and the regulars' names pay off in ch5. Johnny ch4 has no 夜の: they reach the diner at sunset. Johnny ch5's summary now says the foghorn broke (see below).
- **Story logic:** in Maine, the foghorn was still sounding, so ringing the old bell had no reason. Now the foghorn coughs once and goes quiet. In cafe ch4, Dom ran to Nook's Cranny while Timmy and Tommy were drinking cocoa in the café. He now runs home: "a fridge full of milk at home, for my shakes". Kicks asked "did you ever write *back* to her", but Ruth never wrote to him; it's now "write to her".
- **Calendar (Johnny):** Boston in January → **March**. Sugar on snow is a sugaring-season (March) tradition, and Japan, the desert and Maine's "this winter" then fall in the next winter, which fits the mikan. Taiwan to Boston went from three weeks to "a month" (via Panama). Taipei isn't a deep-sea port, so the ship "docked near Taipei" (Keelung).
- **Naturalness:** Someplace (one word). "a very large coffee" (it was "a cup of very large coffee"). "A little bird told me" (it was "I heard a little bird say"). "marshmallows to roast" (they were already roasted). "rent it month to month" (rent is always paid monthly). "a good start". "a catalog" (it was catalogs, then "the catalog"). "tightened everything in sight" (it was found/find). "latte" (an American wouldn't order a "café latte"). "extra hot" (it was "a little too hot"). "sat at the counter". "shone" and "sparkled" (they were "shined"). Redd's tts: "What'd I tell ya" ("Whadda" means "what do").
- **Level:** "things of questionable origin" became "things nobody asked too many questions about".
- **Audio:** after "The Litle Kettle", the narrator now adds "Little, with only one T", so a listener can hear the misspelling.
- **Gloss:** "have something against" = 〜を きらって いる (it was うらみが ある, too strong).

## Cultural check (fine as written)

- **Taipei:** the night market by the river with a temple at one end is Raohe, with pepper buns in a clay oven and the long line. Stinky tofu comes with pickled cabbage.
- **Vermont:** sugar on snow with a plain donut and a sour pickle.
- **Seto Inland Sea:** the hill town with cats on its steps, tatami rooms on the ferry, Setouchi lemons, Kusama's pumpkin.
- **Route 66:** the EAT sign, the stools, "hon", bottomless coffee, pie à la mode, tipping.
- **Maine:** the lobster roll in a buttered, toasted bun, red and green running lights.

## For the user to decide

- **Poetic license, left as is:** one ferry passes both Kusama's pumpkin (Naoshima) and lemon slopes (closer to Onomichi). Most Maine lobster shacks close in winter (the "froze outside" joke depends on it). Real lighthouses have been automated since the 1990s.
- **An open thread:** Johnny never says whether he wrote to Ruth (ch2's "Not yet"). One line in ch5 could close it. Optional.
- **When it's voiced:** check how the US voice says "mikan", "Litle" and "à la mode".
