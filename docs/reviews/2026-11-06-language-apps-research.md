# Conversational English apps: what to copy for a listening game (JP adults, B1+ to B2)

Revised 2026-11-06 with live sources. Tags: [V] fetched and checked; [M] from memory (coe.int returned 403); [U] unverified.

## 1. Duolingo (2025–2026)

**Path.** In August 2022 the old skill tree was replaced by one linear path [V: Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duolingo; https://blog.duolingo.com/new-duolingo-home-screen-design/].
- Each circle on the path is a **level**. Levels form **units**, and each unit header names a goal (e.g. "get directions") and links to the unit's **Guidebook**. Stories and practice sit inside the path [V: same post].
- Units sit inside **sections**. English-course section and unit counts are [U], so I dropped my earlier "~8–9".

**Duolingo Score** [V: https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-score/; https://blog.duolingo.com/goldilocks-and-the-cefr-levels-which-proficiency-level-is-just-right/]
- Scale 0–160, the same as the Duolingo English Test.
- Bands: 0–9 very early A1; 10–19 early A1; 20–29 high A1; 30–59 A2; **60–79 early B1; 80–99 high B1; 100–114 early B2; 115–129 high B2**; 130–160 C1–C2.
- The most popular courses top out at **129 (end of B2)**.
- Shown for English, Spanish and French, from Unit 1 on.
- *Correction:* my first draft's bands (B1 60–89, B2 90–119) were wrong.

**Grammar** is mostly implicit: it comes from patterns plus Guidebook tips. A Duolingo-funded 2023 study found English learners "did not significantly learn much grammar" [V via Wikipedia; primary source not fetched].

**Listening and speaking** [V: "How Duolingo teaches listening skills", 5 May 2026, https://blog.duolingo.com/covering-all-the-bases-duolingos-approach-to-listening-skills/]
- Audio comes with text early in a unit, then alone. Exercises: matching pairs, missing word, tap what you hear, plus a turtle (slow) button.
- **DuoRadio**: audio-only podcast episodes, listed for 8 target languages. Its presence in the English course is [U].
- **Practice tab**: Words, Speak, Listen, Mistakes and Stories.

**Adventures** (24 Sep 2024): video-game-like scenes inside the path. You walk a board and finish an everyday task: ordering coffee, groceries, a cinema ticket, directions, a doctor's receptionist. Mistakes get "immersive feedback" that redirects you rather than correcting you. At launch it was only for English→French and **Spanish→English** [V: https://blog.duolingo.com/adventures/].

**Max** [V: https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-max/; https://blog.duolingo.com/video-call/]
- **Roleplay** (Mar 2023): chat with app characters, then get AI feedback on accuracy and complexity.
- **Video Call with Lily** (Sep 2024, English among its languages): calls of 1–3 minutes. Lily adapts to level, repeats or slows down on request, and **does not correct grammar**.

**Personalisation.** Two systems, now cited separately:
- **Half-life regression** (Settles & Meeder, ACL 2016) estimates the half-life of each word in a learner's memory. It cut recall-prediction error by 45%+ against baselines and raised daily engagement by 12% [V: https://research.duolingo.com/papers/settles.acl16.pdf].
- **Birdbrain** is a separate model. It estimates the learner's ability and the exercise's difficulty, then filters the Session Generator's exercise pool to the right difficulty. In A/B tests learners learned more and came back more. [V: https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain/].
- *Correction:* my first draft said Birdbrain grew out of HLR. The Birdbrain post doesn't say that.

**Evidence** [V: citations via Wikipedia]
- Jiang et al. 2021 (Duolingo report DRR-21-03): **"Finishing half of B1 on Duolingo comparable to five university semesters in reading and listening"**. https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/Duolingo_whitepaper_language_read_listen_2021.pdf
- Jiang et al. 2022, *Foreign Language Annals* 54(4): learners who finished the beginner course matched **four semesters** of university reading and listening. https://doi.org/10.1111/flan.12600
- Colombia/Spain 2023 whitepapers (Duolingo-hosted): Duolingo learners beat classroom learners on proficiency **except listening**. https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/RodriguezFuentes_etal_whitepaper_language_proficiency_Columbia_2023.pdf ; https://duolingo-papers.s3.amazonaws.com/reports/GonzalezFernandez_whitepaper_language_proficiency_Spain_2023.pdf
- None of these measure speaking or conversation. My first draft's "speaking weaker" claim is [U] as a research finding.
- Criticism [V: Wikipedia]: gamification invites gaming the system, and the April 2025 "AI-first" memo caused protest cancellations and reports of falling quality.

**Takeaway.** Duolingo's conversation value lies in Adventures, Video Call and Roleplay. The core path is still sentence-level, and its own research is about reading and listening.

## 2. Conversation-first competitors

- **Babbel** [V: https://www.babbel.com/the-babbel-method; https://www.babbel.com/]
  - ~6-minute lessons built on real-situation dialogues; grammar explained in context; CEFR can-do tasks.
  - Spaced repetition of vocabulary *chunks*, resurfaced mistakes, unit recaps, an AI conversation partner.
  - *Correction:* my earlier "6-step intervals" isn't on Babbel's site [U].
- **Busuu** [V: https://www.busuu.com/en/it-works/courses]
  - CEFR A1–B2 (English to C1); scenario conversations; native-speaker community feedback on written and spoken answers; adaptive review of weak words.
- **Pimsleur** [V: https://www.pimsleur.com/the-pimsleur-method]
  - **Anticipation** (ask, pause, confirm), **Graduated Interval Recall**, a limited **core vocabulary**, and every new item inside a conversation. Lesson length and counts are [M].
- **Speak** [V: https://www.speak.com/]
  - **Learn → Practice → Apply**: situation *patterns*, repeated until automatic, then AI tutor conversation. Custom lessons and mistake review. Strong in Japan (JP site).

**Shared pattern:** situation → model dialogue → chunk/pattern extraction → controlled practice → freer task → spaced review of chunks.

## 3. Core Inventory and CEFR, B1 and B2

**Source:** British Council–EAQUALS *Core Inventory for General English* (2010), with B1 and B2 columns taken from the summary table and the exponent pages [V: https://www.eaquals.org/wp-content/uploads/EAQUALS_British_Council_Core_Curriculum_April2011.pdf].

**B1 functions:** checking understanding; describing experiences and events; describing feelings and emotions; describing places; expressing opinions, agreeing and disagreeing; initiating and closing conversation; managing interaction (interrupting, changing topic, resuming or continuing).

**B2 functions:**
- critiquing; describing experiences, feelings, hopes and plans; developing an argument; abstract ideas; opinion and justification; reaction; speculating; synthesising and evaluating information;
- inviting another speaker in; interacting informally (interest, sympathy, surprise); taking the initiative.

**Discourse markers.**
- B1 categories: connecting words for cause, effect and contrast; sequential past-time linkers. Exponents:
  - *then, after that, finally; on the other hand, however*; informal *Right. / Really? / Well, anyway… / Yes, I suppose so.*;
  - *Sorry to interrupt you but… / By the way… / Anyway, what was I saying?*
- B2 categories: the B1 categories plus *although / in spite of / despite*, *subsequently*, and markers to structure formal speech (*To begin…, Furthermore, Moreover, Consequently, Regarding…, In conclusion*). Exponents:
  - *Hang on! / That reminds me… / Before I forget… / Go on. / What do you reckon? / Really? Tell me more. / That's awful. What a shame! / So what you're really saying is…*

**Grammar.**
- B1: intensifiers (*too, enough*); complex question tags; 2nd and 3rd conditionals; future continuous; modals *must/can't* (deduction), *might/may*, *should have/might have*, *must/have to*; past continuous and perfect; present perfect (continuous) vs past simple; reported speech; simple passive; extended phrasal verbs; *will/going to* for prediction.
- B2: future perfect (continuous); mixed conditionals; *can't have / needn't have*; deduction and speculation; narrative tenses; passives; past perfect continuous; relative clauses; *wish*; *would* for past habits.

**Vocabulary and topics.** Vocabulary at both levels: collocation and colloquial language; B1 adds town and shopping, and travel and services (our domains). Topics at B1: books, education, film, leisure, media, news and lifestyles. B2 adds the arts.

**CEFR Companion Volume (2020) listening scales** [M]: coe.int returned 403, so these are recalled and need checking against https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4.
- *Overall oral comprehension*: B1 main points of clear standard speech on familiar matters; B1+ general message and specific details on everyday or job topics; B2 main ideas of complex speech, following signposted extended discourse.
- *Conversation between others*: B1 main points of a clear discussion around them; B2 catches much of it with effort; B2+ keeps up with animated conversation.
- *Audio media*: B1 main points of slow, clear recordings; B2 standard recordings, including speaker viewpoints and attitudes.
- *Announcements*: B1 detailed directions; B2 announcements at normal speed.

## 4. Recommended structure for our app

**Levels and units.**
- Four levels, mapped onto Duolingo's verified bands:

  | Our level | CEFR | Duolingo Score |
  |---|---|---|
  | B1 | early B1 | 60–79 |
  | B1+ | high B1 | 80–99 |
  | B2- | early B2 | 100–114 |
  | B2 | high B2 | 115–129 |

- About 24 units per level, in **6 life domains**: Friends & life, Travel, Work, Shopping & services, Events, Places & getting around.
- Each unit has 5 lessons plus a review, at about 6 minutes per lesson (Babbel's length).
- The listening goal climbs from following one clear speaker (B1), to details (B1+), to discussion around you (B2-), to animated talk and attitude (B2).

**Unit template**
- **Can-do** (Core Inventory function plus situation), with a Japanese gloss.
- **Key phrases**: 8–12 chunks tagged by function, 1 grammar exponent carried implicitly, and 2 discourse markers from the Inventory lists.
- **Listening progression:**
  1. *Gist*: a 60–90 s natural dialogue (real speed, varied voices). Answer who, where, and what the problem is.
  2. *Detail and attitude*: a timed question, then how the speaker feels (the CV B2 "viewpoints and attitudes").
  3. *Chunk catch*: pick the phrase heard among near-distractors. Drill connected speech (*wanna, d'you, gonna*, linking, weak forms). This is the critical skill for Japanese ears; it is my judgement, not something the sources state.
  4. *Respond*: Pimsleur anticipation. Hear a line, pause, choose or say the reply, then hear the model answer.
  5. *Mission*: a branching scene (Adventures and Speak Apply). Scored on understanding and appropriate replies, with redirecting rather than correcting feedback (as in Adventures and Lily).
- **Review**: HLR-style per-chunk intervals, Birdbrain-style difficulty filtering, a weekly "radio" episode recycling 4 units, and a mistakes queue.

### A. Making and changing plans with friends (Friends & life, B1+)
- Can-do: *I can suggest, arrange, postpone and cancel plans, giving reasons.*
- Grammar: present continuous for arrangements. Markers: *actually, anyway*.
- Phrases: *Are you free on Saturday? / Do you fancy grabbing a coffee? / How about we meet at…? / That works for me. / Can we push it back to 7? / Something's come up. / I'm going to have to cancel. / Can I take a rain check? / No worries, another time.*
- Mission: voice notes change the plan twice. Catch the final time and place, then reply.

### B. Sorting out a problem at a hotel (Travel, B1+)
- Can-do: *I can explain a problem politely, ask for a solution and confirm what will happen.*
- Grammar: present perfect (*The AC has stopped working*); *could/would* requests. Markers: *I'm afraid, the thing is*.
- Phrases: *I'm sorry to bother you, but… / There seems to be a problem with… / The air conditioning isn't working. / Would it be possible to change rooms? / Could you send someone up to have a look? / How long will that take? / So just to confirm… / I appreciate your help.*
- Mission: a phone call with a fast receptionist. Choose between an upgrade and a discount, then confirm it.

### C. Small talk with coworkers (Work, B1+ to B2-)
- Can-do: *I can start, keep going and politely end casual conversations at work* (Inventory: initiating and closing; interacting informally).
- Grammar: present perfect vs past simple for news; question tags. Markers: *by the way, that reminds me*.
- Phrases: *How was your weekend? / Did you get up to anything fun? / Busy week, huh? / Have you tried the new place downstairs? / Really? Tell me more. / By the way, are you going to the team lunch? / Anyway, I'd better get back to it. / Catch you later.*
- Mission: two colleagues overlapping in the kitchen. Catch a detail, pick the follow-up question that keeps the talk going, then close.

**Principles:** real voices at natural speed; chunks before grammar; spaced review of phrases; every unit ends in a task; feedback redirects; connected speech taught explicitly.
